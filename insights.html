01 On air
Live commerce8 min read

Designing live commerce as a service, not an event

Live selling works best when it is treated as a repeatable customer journey rather than an occasional broadcast. Preparation should begin with the product mix, demonstration order, pricing logic and support coverage for every session. Hosts need concise product prompts, credible demonstrations and clear ways to handle questions about variants, delivery or returns. Teams should also plan how live demand moves into inventory allocation, fulfilment and post-session follow-up. A useful review considers the entire flow: content that attracts the right viewers, staff who can keep the room moving, and order handling that protects the customer experience after the broadcast ends. Consistent scheduling and documented playbooks make each session easier to improve without losing the warmth that makes live commerce distinctive.

02 Reconciled
Operations10 min read

Cash on delivery reconciliation without the weekly scramble

Cash on delivery can create a settlement problem when marketplace payouts, courier collections, failed deliveries and customer refunds are recorded in different places. A disciplined process begins by defining one source for order-level financial data and a common set of reference details for every transaction. Teams then need to match expected cash against collected cash, investigate failed attempts and account for cancellations, disputes and partial refunds. The final view should explain not only what has been collected, but why a difference exists and who needs to resolve it. A shared reconciliation calendar helps prevent ageing items from being overlooked. Over time, the process also reveals useful operational signals, such as repeated delivery failures, confusing product pages or service interactions that create avoidable cash collection issues. Clear ownership is as important as the spreadsheet itself.

03 Campaign room
Growth9 min read

Mega campaigns: preparing the business before the countdown

A marketplace mega campaign can concentrate demand, but only if the surrounding operation is ready. Inventory, pricing, creative assets, promotion mechanics and fulfilment capacity should be reviewed together rather than owned by separate teams. A useful planning phase maps customer demand to stock by location and expected delivery window, while establishing approval paths for issues that may emerge once volumes rise. Creative needs enough range to respond to different audiences, yet every version should retain a consistent proposition. Teams should also agree how ads, live sessions, creator activity and marketplace promotions will influence one another. Tracking should distinguish campaign traffic from genuinely incremental sales, with a daily view of spend, stock, service pressure and conversion. The strongest campaigns are not simply larger. They are calmer, more visible and easier for every team to manage.

04 Discoverable
Growth7 min read

Listing SEO that helps customers choose with confidence

Marketplace search is not a separate marketing channel; it is the meeting point between machine-readable catalogue data and a customer's decision. Strong listing SEO starts with a clear product definition: what the item is, who it suits, which features matter and how it differs from nearby alternatives. Search terms should appear naturally in the title and supporting copy without sacrificing readability. Images, videos and attribute fields should reinforce the same message, because customers often move between them before making a choice. A practical review also checks whether pricing, promotions and delivery information are easy to understand. Listings should be maintained as products evolve, with outdated stock, discontinued variants and ambiguous descriptions corrected promptly. Over time, search-term performance, customer questions and conversion patterns can guide which product attributes deserve more detail. The aim is not to stuff a page, but to remove uncertainty.

05 Across borders
Cross-border11 min read

Cross-border growth begins with the customer promise

Selling across Southeast Asia is more than extending a domestic catalogue to new storefronts. Each destination introduces different expectations around delivery time, duties, returns, language and customer care. A reliable cross-border model starts by deciding what can be promised consistently and where local execution is required. Product information should make size, materials, compatibility and usage expectations explicit, especially when a purchase cannot be handled in person. Fulfilment pathways need to be tested against realistic order profiles rather than ideal conditions, while service teams should know which questions they can answer and which need escalation. Inventory allocation should balance market potential against the risk of long delivery journeys. A simple, honest customer promise often performs better than an ambitious one that creates uncertainty. Regional expansion becomes more manageable when localisation is treated as an operating discipline, not a final translation pass.

06 Assisted work
AI operations8 min read

Where AI can genuinely assist marketplace operations

AI is most useful in marketplace operations when it reduces repetitive interpretation while keeping people accountable for judgement. Practical starting points include grouping customer questions by intent, suggesting listing improvements, summarising campaign notes and flagging reconciliation differences for review. These tasks benefit from clear source material, defined boundaries and a person who can check the output. Prompts should reflect the brand's voice, product facts and service standards rather than relying on broad assumptions. Teams should also measure quality by task: Is a suggested answer complete? Does a listing change remain accurate? Has a financial discrepancy been escalated appropriately? The aim should be to make the operating model more responsive, not to remove context. Data permissions, sensitive information, escalation paths and audit trails matter from the beginning. AI works best as a capable assistant inside a well-designed process.
